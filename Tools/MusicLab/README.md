# Music Lab

From the project root, run `node Tools/MusicLab/serve.mjs` and open [Music Lab](http://127.0.0.1:8766/). The server reads the game's MP3s and cue sheets directly. An optional numeric argument selects another local port.

Click the waveform to seek to that time. While paused, the click sets the start point for Play. With the waveform focused, the left and right arrow keys move the playhead by one second.

Choose a track and press Play. Intro runs into Flight. Section and song changes look for the destination’s opening chord within ten seconds of playback. Outro has a 0.35-second limit and a 0.24-second crossfade. The queue shows the chosen chord or fallback and the cut time. **Preview the change** moves the audition playhead to two beats before a queued section change. Boss repeats a long high-energy passage at its original tempo with a 4.5 dB low shelf at 145 Hz. The outro releases the bass over one beat. The short Stage Complete cue plays through once.

Choose **Intro**, **Flight**, **Boss** or **Outro** under **Section markers**. Drag either waveform handle or use the arrows beside **Start** and **End** to step one four-beat bar. The file edges are available for pickups and tails. Each section must include one full bar. **Hear ending** auditions the last two beats and what follows them. Intro hands off to Flight, Flight and Boss loop, and Outro plays out. **Save for game** writes all edited sections for the song together. The next Unity play session and future builds read those markers.

**Boss (fast)** is an audition-only 6% tempo lift at the original pitch. Opening an adaptive recording prepares both tempos. Switching between Boss and Boss (fast) keeps the current playhead and kick, with a 12 ms crossfade. Both crossfade into the original outro with no speed ramp.

Boss adds a hard kick on every beat in the song’s cue sheet. The game and both boss previews use the same mono mix of **Monster Kick 127** from FL Studio 20’s `Data/Patches/Packs/Drums/Kicks` folder. Its level is 4 dB above the original boss mix. Each hit ducks the backing music by up to 1 dB with a 2 ms attack and a 55 ms release. In the fast preview the hits move closer together and keep their original pitch.

## Cue analysis

With Node 18+, NumPy, FFmpeg and ffprobe installed, run `npm ci --prefix Tools/MusicLab` once. Run `python Tools/MusicLab/analyze.py --chords-only` to refresh the harmony on the existing beat grids and section markers. Append MP3 basenames to analyze individual tracks. Omit `--chords-only` for a full cue analysis. The beat and phrase analysis uses audio onsets and recurring bar sequences. Plains uses a 169 BPM hint and follows the pulse of the shipped recording. Full analysis also records the MP3 lead-in so Unity can seek on the same timeline when its importer retains the encoder padding.

Chordino estimates tuning, separates note fundamentals from their overtones, and decodes a sequence of chords from bass and treble evidence. The cue sheets retain sevenths, inversions and unpitched gaps. Changes within 100 ms and 23% of a beat of the measured pulse can align to it. Offbeat changes retain their detected times. The detector runs offline when generating the cue sheets. [Chordino method](https://isophonics.net/nnls-chroma) · [Music Lab's Chordino implementation](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@audio/mir-chordino)

The cue times are seconds in the original recording. Reanalysis keeps hand-picked sections on the updated bar grid. Chord matching uses the chord sounding at the destination’s saved IN marker, or its first pitched chord if it opens with a silent pickup. The outgoing change must match the full label, including its quality and any detected bass inversion. When no matching change fits inside the applicable time limit and the outgoing section, use the next progression ending or the selected OUT, whichever comes first. If both fall beyond the limit, use the last available bar line within it, then a beat or the deadline. Flight can pass through the boss passage during a long level. A boss request engages the bass boost. Chord and phrase labels are listening estimates. The final passage serves as the outro for recordings that end without a composed fade.
