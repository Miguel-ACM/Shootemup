# Industrial fleet and equipment

The new default presentation renders at native resolution. Full-screen dithering, vignette and vertex wobble are disabled; low-resolution textures and flat mesh lighting supply the retro look. F9 remains an optional comparison filter.

The shared live HUD in L1 and PSX_Showcase keeps score and multiplier progress, with vertical armour/reactor bars on the left and a boss hull bar when needed. Headers, numerical resource labels and control hints are removed. The old gameplay UI controllers remain active behind hidden instrument graphics, so pickups, boss logic, menus and item dialogs keep their existing references. Pause/title menus and item dialogs use pixel fonts and the steel/brass palette.

Four textured enemy meshes replace the interceptor, waver, Biggie and stage-one boss visuals. They contain 100, 116, 168 and 272 triangles. Biggie is now a twin-keel gunboat with separate engine nacelles and long cannons. Their original 2D collision shapes, movers, firing patterns, health and drops remain in use; a child visual mirrors damage flashes and forwards visibility events.

Seventeen equipment meshes cover every current pickup and upgrade type. Each has 28–240 triangles and trim UVs into the new `Assets/Art/IndustrialExpansion/EquipmentAtlas.png`. Health is a red medical kit, energy a blue battery and score a yellow coin. Upgrades have literal silhouettes: cratered moons, a coffee mug with coffee and steam, rabbit paw, rifle, protein tub, helper robot, miniature ship, magnet, lightning bolt, arm and bent spoon. Pickups, upgrade choices and all four companion prefabs rotate their visual model independently of their collision body. Equipped active items hide the model. OBJ/MTL sources and individual Unity renders are next to this file; the Unity mesh assets are in `Assets/Art/IndustrialExpansion`.

The player canopy UV region has a bright blue material treatment. The boss visual is sized from its polygon collider bounds; the previous fallback incorrectly made it one unit wide. Original collider geometry, enemy statistics, firing settings and movement remain unchanged.

Item descriptions now use a centred screen-space card with readable wrapped text and a short alpha fade. Hidden cards disable their canvas completely. The boss warning streams twelve rows of red WARNING text in alternating directions. A 405 × 720 RectMask2D clips the entire wash and text to the portrait playfield, leaving the black sidebars and side instruments clear. It clears after three seconds. `ReadabilityAudit.json` checks panel centring/hiding, warning clipping/clearing, boss world width, nearest-only shop selection, item flight, single collection, rarity orbits and player/enemy impact bursts, while actual Unity captures are in `ArtSource/PSXPreview`.

Player and companion rounds, small/medium/large enemy rounds and the expanding wave use point-filtered sprite quads and one shared unlit material. There are no projectile MeshRenderers or tracer TrailRenderers on these six prefabs. Allied rounds are blue; enemy rounds use orange circular or cyan diamond silhouettes with contrasting cores. This reduces render geometry and removes per-projectile trail objects. The existing Instantiate/Destroy projectile lifetime still applies; this pass does not add pooling or establish a frame-rate benchmark.

`GeometryAudit.json` checks all 21 meshes for collapsed geometry and UV triangles. `ArtSource/PSXPreview/GameplayAudit.json` records real L1 play checks for pickups, model selection, rotation, active-item hiding and the live HUD. The temporary runtime test object is added to an unsaved scene and is not included in L1. `PSX_Showcase.unity` runs automatic firing and a sample fleet; F8 restores manual controls. Its autopilot restores health for the visual demonstration only.

Regenerate with **Tools > PSX Player > Install industrial fleet and UI** outside Play mode. The older player/visual-pass installers are historical and will restore their original visual setup. Backups for this revision are in `MigrationBackup/BeforeIndustrialExpansion`.

The Freight Warden is now a 272-triangle armored carrier with separate heavy engine pods, four long guns, stepped bridge, recessed hangar and central reactor. Its original gameplay components and polygon collider remain intact. Moons use continuous longitude/latitude UVs on an 80-triangle sphere and a painted lunar tile with maria, crater bowls and raised rims; the three-moon mesh totals 240 triangles. The shared equipment atlas also has recessed panels, rivets, scuffs, ceramic details and dull brass. Red medical and blue battery faceplates sit in gray metal housings.

Approaching shop options attracts the closest item within the same magnet radius as ordinary pickups. A shop claim prevents another option from collecting while the selected item accelerates toward the player. Rewards and wave progression happen once, on arrival. Spinning shop items emit small rarity-colored orbit motes; resource pickups use red/blue/gold motes. Pickup bursts and enemy/player hit sparks share one bounded 512-particle world-space buffer; each item aura has a 64-particle limit. Bullets remain sprite quads.

Rendering repair: the orthographic gameplay camera now sits 40 units behind the gameplay plane, with the same Z offset applied to CameraShaker.RestPositionOffset so shaking does not reset it into the models. Framing and gameplay positions remain unchanged. The expanded near/far range contains raised boss parts and spinning item geometry. Baby You and Twerp visuals now have explicit readable sizes independent of their original small colliders, and their idle rotation keeps their faces visible.

Electricity and Angry Moon beams use the supported Universal2D additive shader, blue-white line colors and explicit Bullet sorting. LineDrawer initializes in Awake so it can draw immediately when instantiated, and electricity stays visible for its existing 0.1-second shot lifetime. Beam owners clean up their line objects. Enemy sparks receive the real incoming bullet velocity or electricity segment direction and emit into a narrow forward cone.

The rarity effect is a soft radial halo with drifting wisps, rendered by PSXAura.shader and anchored to the item during attraction. Unchosen items disable collection immediately, then rise, shrink and fade over 0.48 seconds before removal. RenderingAudit.json tests the camera after camera shake, model depth bounds, follower sizes, aura emission, laser/electricity visibility and damage, forward spark velocities, and the actual shop collection/dismissal path. RenderingRegressionProbe is an unsaved editor-only test; normal L1 is reopened afterward.

The follower history buffer also handles resizing before its first recorded movement. Adding a second follower previously entered a sentinel loop with head=-1 and threw an IndexOutOfRangeException, preventing companion registration from completing. Resizing now preserves newest-to-oldest samples and pads older slots with the current default position; the rendering regression covers both untouched and populated history buffers.
