# Brief: hangar props over the Tunnel district floor (top-down shoot-em-up)

The game's hub screen between runs is a hangar bay INSIDE the game's Tunnel district. The attached image is the Tunnel district in game: a dark, desaturated teal-gray industrial shaft, a central floor channel of dark metal deck plates with small recessed hatches, and side walls with dark vertical recesses, pipes and columns. The engine will draw that exact tunnel floor and walls behind your props. A previous standalone hangar was rejected because it looked like a bright, flat parking lot (cream arrows, bright hazard paint, light gray concrete) that did not belong to the game. Your props must look like they were built into this tunnel: same very dark teal-gray steel, low contrast, small and sparse warm amber and cyan lamps, chunky bevelled industrial forms.

Write ONE Blender 5.x Python script named `model.py` in the current directory. Do not run Blender yourself. The script must:

1. Clear the scene and build only from bpy primitives, bmesh and applied modifiers (no external files, no addons).
2. Coordinates: X right, Y screen up, +Z toward the camera above (orthographic camera straight down). The visible area is X -5..+5, Y -8.9..+8.9. The tunnel's open floor channel runs down the middle from about x = -2.9 to +2.9; the side walls are from |x| = 2.9 to 5.
3. Model ONLY these props, with NO floor slab, no background plane and nothing covering the rest of the area (the tunnel shows through everywhere else):
   - LEFT: four ship docking cradles, centered at (-3.4, +4.2), (-3.4, +1.4), (-3.4, -1.4), (-3.4, -4.2): each an octagonal or round landing platform about 1.6 across, slightly raised (z 0 to 0.12), mounted on the left wall with a docking arm/clamp reaching from the wall (x = -5) to the platform, a thin ring of small amber lamps around its rim and a dark centre. Keep the platform top flat and empty: the game parks a ship on it.
   - RIGHT: the shop: three display plinths centered at (+3.4, +3.4), (+3.4, +0.6), (+3.4, -2.2): each a raised octagonal pedestal about 1.1 across with a thin cyan glowing rim, set into a wall-mounted counter/rack structure that runs along the right wall from y = -3.2 to +4.4 (shelves, a vending cabinet, small crates). Keep the plinth tops empty: the game places items there.
   - TOP: the exit: a heavy pair of blast doors slid open across the channel at y from +6.8 to +8.9 (door slabs pulled into the side walls, toothed edges), a dark open shaft between them, and two warning beacons. A few small amber guide lights down the channel centre (x = 0) from y = +6.5 to -6 (small dots, not stripes).
   - BOTTOM (y from -8.9 to -6.6): a low workshop ledge along the bottom: a tool bench, a couple of crates and barrels, a crane hook, all dark. Keep (0, -5.8) clear.
4. Materials: flat Base Colors only. Use the tunnel's palette: dark teal-grays between (0.03,0.045,0.05) and (0.20,0.25,0.25), a little desaturated rust (0.22,0.13,0.09) and dull brass (0.27,0.23,0.13). Materials whose name starts with `glow_` are lamps drawn at full brightness: use them sparingly for the pad lamp rings (amber 0.95,0.62,0.25), plinth rims (cyan 0.25,0.75,0.85), beacons and guide dots. No cream, no white, no bright yellow/black hazard stripes, no painted arrows, no text.
5. Budget: 3000-15000 triangles, flat shading, chunky readable shapes with bevels; avoid noisy greebles and rows of identical buttons.
6. Join everything into ONE mesh object named `Model`, apply transforms and modifiers. Export `model.obj` (+ .mtl) next to the script with `bpy.ops.wm.obj_export` (export materials, forward_axis='Y', up_axis='Z').
7. Render `top.png` (450x800) next to the script with Cycles (CPU, 32 samples): orthographic camera straight down framing exactly X -5..5, Y -8.9..8.9, world colour (0.06,0.08,0.09), sun from the upper left plus a soft fill. Find the script directory from `__file__`, falling back to the path after `--` in sys.argv.

When done, reply with one short paragraph describing the design.
