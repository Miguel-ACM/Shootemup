# Heaven and Void

Space and Underwater now offer Hell or Heaven. Either fifth-tier district leads directly to Void after its boss reward. Void ends the campaign after its own reward. District travel retains the ship, equipment, wallet, score and seed. Existing simulator contracts still end after their contracted boss.

Heaven retains the ten-wave district structure, opening item, two encounter decisions and earned miniboss reward. Its five regular enemies each have a modeled silhouette, explicit weapon lips and three scored formation parts:

| Enemy | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Choir Lancer | Sculpted ivory pinions and a recessed downward lance |
| Aegis Reliquary | Curved shield shells and twin vaulted batteries; alternating lance and accelerating arrows |
| Prism Cantor | Suspended crystal in an open arched cradle; outward beams and ceiling-rebounding needles |
| Votive Spear | Needle prow and curved pennant blades; stacked, diverging aimed arrows |
| Throne Barge | Cathedral crown and recessed siege aperture; central beam and shoulder canopy |
| Dawn Gatekeeper | Sculpted paladin with vaulted armor and layered pinions; three mirrored moves |
| Seraph Judicator | Ophanim angel: a living central eye inside three independently rotating golden wheels covered with eyes; Gates of Dawn, First Trumpet, Firmament, Judgment Pendulum, Last Trumpet and Broken Covenant |

Siege lasers reuse the existing pooled PulseLaser, continuous beam shader, shared bloom and analytic contact check. Their final footprint is visible during charging; width expands over 0.65 enemy-tempo seconds and damage follows the same envelope. Existing laser envelopes remain unchanged. Heaven's warm amber-orange fire contrasts against ivory cloud banks and golden ramparts.

Miguel's later Heaven-boss direction replaces the winged spacecraft with an eye-covered, ringed angel. Its three interlocking gold wheels carry 36 sculpted eyes around a larger pearl-white central eye. The wheels turn in different directions with slow precession; lasers remain attached to their chosen eye for the entire charge and release, while needle volleys leave the outer eyes. The core and individual eyes share boss health, with open collision space between them. The health tuning, phase count, card scores and approved laser sounds are preserved. `VerifyHeavenVoid.RunAngel` reviews the revised model and all three phases in isolation, including moving contacts, attached beams and the continuous finale.

Heaven's revised scenery uses asymmetric deep cumulus banks, marble terraces, broken gilded arches, colonnades, curled torn banners and planted spears. The background opens into blue sky between brighter cloud banks with restrained shafts of warm light. Curved shared-vertex surfaces and pearl/gold shading replace the former flat fleet plates. The angel's eyes now have curved lids, corneas and recessed pupils; its wheels use interwoven golden strands.

Void contains only its boss. Normal and Hard have four sculpted forms: the horned Veiled Monarch, the hooked Severing Saint, the many-toothed Devourer and the fracture-winged Unmade. Easy skips the Devourer. Each form has two distinct attack scores. Witnesses and Funeral Psalm use rising bursting shells, hanging pearls and splitting giant tears. Severing and Cut the Firmament use returning blades, reflected ceiling shots, charged lasers and paired helixes. Hunger and False Stars use destructible plasma, committed missiles, upward teeth, thrown mines, carriers and lunging stars. World Without Edges and Last Equation use four-direction folding curtains and interleaved reflected teeth, returning blades and delayed splits. Both cards of every form put threats into the top of the arena. All thirteen delivery types retain their existing approved artwork and violet palette.

Each transition hides the boss for exactly 15 active-play seconds, suspending the timer during pause/time stop. Miguel's 5 November correction replaces the mixed perimeter attacks with one side for the entire disappearance: top, right, then bottom. Two charged chevrons mark the actual sources on that edge. Funeral fans open into paired feathers; scissor brocade hinges an ordered comb; unmaking interleaves scalloped fast crests and slow troughs. Each has a continuous moving slit and mirrored timing. Rounds cannot materialize within 1.6 world units of the player. The boss rejects damage and homing while hidden, clears the barrage at reappearance and has a settling interval before its next attack. Damage is clamped at phase boundaries so large bursts cannot skip a form. All forms use the established phase break, finale and boss-reward pipeline.

The same correction strengthens both Heaven and Void bosses without adding health. Heaven's main scores now release upper and lower feather lobes together, plus six visibly charged apparitions that summon mirrored petals in empty air. The golden wheels tumble on opposed world axes as well as spinning on their own axles. They keep turning during attacks and speed up with each phase; lasers remain bound to their committed eyes, and moving eye collision paths remain intact. Void's visible forms have closer-spaced phrases, broader folds, accelerating answers behind the pearl ribs and synchronized scissor turns. False Stars now uses the intended bounded fan instead of accidentally interpreting its total spread as a per-shard angle.

Space's related correction replaces the small clearance-limited circle with concentric fronts at 2.6-unit intervals throughout the arena. Inward arrows counterturn on neighboring fronts, and dark stars use alternate circles. The tell draws only the visible arcs inside the combat rectangle. First-phase accretion and the final Big Crunch retain the moving central target, gravity and a rotating escape seam. Births near the player are omitted, with the larger clearance retained for dark stars. The intermediate expulsion and supernova score is preserved.

The pattern-design reference is [ZUN's explanation of giving attacks a distinctive identity](https://en.touhougarakuta.com/article/specialtaidan_zun_hiroyuki_5-en/): the feather vaults, funeral fabrics and accretion fronts each have their own organizing motion rather than sharing an arbitrary collection of deliveries. `VerifyCelestialPatterns.Run` runs the revised cases in the isolated command-line checkout, measures native projectile origins and complete CPU frames, and exports trajectories for `Tools/review_celestial_patterns.py`. Diagrams are geometry evidence, not gameplay screenshots or Miguel's approval of difficulty.

Void assigns the existing projectile reserve behind the cloud curtain to all thirteen deliveries, retaining the standard-round and giant-orb reserves. The profile accounts for the game's current reserve multiplier and fits the existing global pool budget; it does not increase the limits. Shell ignition is also prepared during pool warming. `CelestialPolishReview` samples complete card cycles, per-delivery peaks, top-band coverage, phase timing, combat allocations and full main-thread frames.

New locally generated laser charge/ignition takes and their original/best versions are retained under `ArtSource/HeavenVoidReview`, with standalone audition pages. After automatic approval review initially blocked import without listening approval, Miguel explicitly chose to keep the approved laser sounds for now. The game uses those existing beam samples with a deeper siege hum; the generated takes remain review-only. Sound Lab selections and its published bank are unchanged. Heaven/Void music is intentionally unset until Miguel supplies tracks; the previous district's song is stopped.

The batch found one shortage: Hard needed 192 simultaneous ricochets against 160 reserved.
